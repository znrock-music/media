Rock Songs About Hating Your Job and the Daily Grind

A song list · 11 songs · updated October 2026

Monday alarm, the same commute, one more deadline. Rock has been writing about the work week since before most of these bands had a hit, from factory floors to appliance stores to the nine-to-five you keep promising to leave. Two songs here are mine, tagged ZN Rock, about the years that go by while you wait for the work to slow down. The rest are by bands you know, with a note on each and a link to play it.

  1. Working Man · Rush

    From Rush's first album (1974). A plain song about a man who works all day and lives for the drink and the weekend, long before the band got known for its longer, stranger material.

    Official video

  2. I Keep Saying I'll Be Happy When · ZN RockZN Rock

    Always one more year and one more round of work first. "Well the work don't slow, there's always one more round." It ends with putting a real date next to the top of the list and calling someone up.

    Lyric videoLyricsSave it on SpotifyApple MusicYouTube MusicAll music apps

  3. Money for Nothing · Dire Straits

    From Brothers in Arms (1985). Mark Knopfler wrote it from the point of view of a worker hauling appliances in a store and grumbling about the musicians on the TV. Sting sings the "I want my MTV" line in the intro, and it went to No. 1 in the US.

    Official video

  4. Factory · Bruce Springsteen

    From Darkness on the Edge of Town (1978). A short, quiet song that watches a man walk out to the factory every morning, told from a son's point of view.

  5. Career Opportunities · The Clash

    From The Clash's first album (1977). A UK punk song about the only jobs on offer being ones nobody wants.

  6. Blue Collar Man (Long Nights) · Styx

    From Pieces of Eight (1978). The narrator is out of work and willing to take whatever job comes, and the song never lets him get comfortable.

  7. Takin' Care of Business · Bachman-Turner Overdrive

    From Bachman-Turner Overdrive II (1973). The narrator rides to work every day and would rather be doing it his own way, and the chorus became one of the most-played rock shout-alongs of the decade.

  8. Nobody Gets to Keep It · ZN RockZN Rock

    A decade of doing what the older, smarter people said would work, then waking up on a Tuesday with the same look on your face. It isn't a job song, but it covers the same disappointment: the thing you were told would make it all worth it didn't hold.

    Lyric videoLyricsAdd it to your Liked SongsApple MusicYouTube MusicAll music apps

  9. Working in the Coal Mine · Devo

    Devo's 1981 cover of the Lee Dorsey hit from 1966, written by Allen Toussaint, recorded for the Heavy Metal film soundtrack. The beat is cheerful and the song is about being worn down.

  10. Workin' for a Livin' · Huey Lewis and the News

    From Picture This (1982). A bar-band shuffle about the daily grind, written from the point of view of the person who shows up every morning anyway.

  11. Dead End Street · The Kinks

    A 1966 single. A man out of work and a family stuck on the same street with no way up, set to a bouncy tune that makes it hit harder.

Questions

What are some good rock songs about hating your job?

Working Man by Rush, Takin' Care of Business by Bachman-Turner Overdrive, Money for Nothing by Dire Straits and Career Opportunities by The Clash are the ones that come up most. Blue Collar Man by Styx is about being out of work.

What are rock songs about working all the time and never getting to enjoy life?

I Keep Saying I'll Be Happy When by ZN Rock is about always needing one more year of work first. Nobody Gets to Keep It by ZN Rock is about chasing something you were told would hold. There is also a longer list of rock songs about chasing happiness.

What are rock songs about the daily grind?

Factory by Bruce Springsteen, Workin' for a Livin' by Huey Lewis and the News and Working in the Coal Mine by Devo all take the daily routine as the subject. There is also a list of rock songs about burnout.
