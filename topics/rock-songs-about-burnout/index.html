Rock Songs About Burnout and Working Too Much

A song list · 14 songs · updated October 2026

Songs for when the work doesn't stop and the life you were saving up for keeps getting pushed to next year. Most of them are rock songs you'll recognise. Three are mine and carry the ZN Rock tag. Under each one is what it's about and where to hear it, and the two playlists wait at the bottom.

  1. Cat's in the Cradle · Harry Chapin

    Chapin's only US No. 1, from Verities & Balderdash (1974). The words began as a poem by his wife Sandy, and it follows a father who's always too busy for his son, until the son grows up just as busy.

    Official video

  2. Movin' Out (Anthony's Song) · Billy Joel

    The opening track on The Stranger (1977). Anthony and the people around him work every hour they can for a house or a Cadillac, and Joel asks what it's worth if the work wrecks your health before you get to enjoy any of it.

    Official video

  3. I Keep Saying I'll Be Happy When · ZN RockZN Rock

    The work never slows down, there's always one more round, and the list of things you'll do someday has sat in a drawer for years. Then a man you came up with dies, nobody at his service mentions anything he made, and you go home and put a date on that list.

    Lyric videoLyricsSave it on SpotifyApple MusicYouTube Music

  4. Running on Empty · Jackson Browne

    The title track of a 1977 album recorded entirely on tour: on stage, backstage, in hotel rooms and on the bus. It's about the endless road, and a life that keeps rolling faster than you can take stock of it.

    Official video

  5. Time · Pink Floyd

    From The Dark Side of the Moon (1973). Roger Waters wrote it at 29, when it hit him that he was no longer getting ready for life. He was already in the middle of it, and it was racing by.

    Official video

  6. Nobody Gets to Keep It · ZN RockZN Rock

    About ten years of doing everything the older, smarter people said would work, then waking up on a Tuesday with the same look on your face. It stops treating the bad days as proof you're doing it wrong and takes the good hours as they come.

    Lyric videoLyricsSave it on SpotifyApple MusicYouTube Music

  7. Working for the Weekend · Loverboy

    The lead single from Get Lucky (1981). Paul Dean got the idea on a Wednesday afternoon walk, when the place was deserted and he figured everyone was at work, waiting for the weekend.

    Official video

  8. Under Pressure · Queen & David Bowie

    Recorded in Montreux, Switzerland, in 1981, and a UK No. 1. It's about the pressure that piles up on ordinary people, with love held up as the way out from under it.

    Official video

  9. Expert On Everything · ZN RockZN Rock

    Not a job, but it eats the hours like one: a take on every war and border on the screen while the sink at home has been broken since July. It ends by handing the whole world back and keeping a smaller job, the one right in front of you.

    Lyric videoLyricsSave it on SpotifyApple MusicYouTube Music

  10. Turn the Page · Bob Seger

    Seger wrote it on tour in 1972 to get down what he called the real weariness of the road. The live take from Live Bullet (1976), recorded at Detroit's Cobo Hall, is the one rock radio made famous.

    Official video

  11. No Surprises · Radiohead

    From OK Computer (1997). Thom Yorke described it as someone who's trying hard to keep it together but can't, sung over a tune so gentle he called it a nursery rhyme.

    Official video

  12. Factory · Bruce Springsteen

    From Darkness on the Edge of Town (1978), and shaped by his father's working life. It follows the men who walk in when the whistle blows and what years of that work take out of them.

    Official video

  13. Working Man · Rush

    From Rush's first album (1974), and the song Cleveland's WMMS played that got the band its first US listeners. Geddy Lee has said it was about anyone who works hard and wants more from life than that.

    Official video

  14. A Hard Day's Night · The Beatles

    The title came from something Ringo Starr said after a day's work that ran on through the night, and John Lennon wrote the song around it in 1964. Working like a dog all day, and the one thing at home that makes it worth it.

    Official video

Questions

What are some rock songs about burnout?

Running on Empty by Jackson Browne, Turn the Page by Bob Seger, Time by Pink Floyd and No Surprises by Radiohead are good places to start. I Keep Saying I'll Be Happy When by ZN Rock is about putting your life off for one more round of work.

What rock songs are about working too much?

Cat's in the Cradle by Harry Chapin is the classic, a father too busy for his son. Movin' Out by Billy Joel, Working for the Weekend by Loverboy and Working Man by Rush are about the grind itself.
